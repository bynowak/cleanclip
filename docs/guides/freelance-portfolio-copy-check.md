# Freelance portfolio copy: a practical pre-launch check

A free ByNowak worksheet for freelance web designers and developers. Use it on your own service page before publishing. You need your current copy, an accurate service scope and one genuine project you can show.

CleanClip can help move your own page text into Markdown for editing. It does not assess your message, rewrite the copy or predict sales. This guide covers the manual questions.

## 1. Name the service before the personality

Read your headline without the screenshot underneath it. Can someone tell what they can hire you to do?

A useful starting sentence is:

**[The service you actually deliver] for [the customer you actually serve].**

Then explain the job in a second sentence:

**I help [customer] [do a specific thing] through [the deliverable you provide].**

These are editable templates, not descriptions of a real client or evidence of improved performance. Replace every bracket and remove any claim you cannot support.

## 2. Make the scope easy to compare

Write four short lines:

- Includes: [your actual deliverables].
- Does not include: [an important exclusion].
- Before work begins: [the material or approval you need].
- Next step: [what the buyer actually does].

If the price is fixed, show the amount, currency and whether it is one-time or recurring. If scope determines the price, explain what information you need before quoting. Do not invent a revision allowance or delivery deadline.

## 3. Make the button describe the next screen

Read the button by itself. A visitor should be able to predict what will happen.

“View the service scope” fits a details page. “Request a website quote” fits an inquiry form. “Buy the copy kit” fits a purchase checkout.

Do not label an email signup “Download now” if the file will not be available on the next screen. If delivery is by email, say so before payment.

## 4. Show proof you can explain honestly

Use a genuine project, repository, working page or deliverable that you have permission to show. Explain your own contribution and what someone can inspect.

Label an own-project demonstration as an own project. Label an untested concept as a concept. Do not turn a template into an invented customer case study or imply that a design produced a measured outcome.

A small, accurate project explanation is more useful than an unsupported claim about results.

## 5. Carry the same offer into a post

Try this editable caption:

**My [service] covers [actual deliverables] for [specific customer]. It is a fit when you need [the job it performs]. Start by sending [the real information your inquiry process needs].**

Show your actual service scope or a permission-cleared project detail beside it. Remove private client information. Keep the action consistent with the page that the post links to.

## 6. Record the buying journey separately

An impression is exposure. An outbound click is an attempt to visit. An inquiry is interest. A confirmed paid order is a sale.

Keep those records separate, with dates and source labels where available. A page with no traffic has not had the same test as a page with many relevant visits and no purchases. Do not declare a winning message from a handful of views.
